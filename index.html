01 / Manifesto

Why I build

Clinical problems become engineering questions.

Medicine is full of systems that work without explaining themselves. A merit list decides where a trainee spends the next four years. A table of susceptibilities lives in a PDF nobody has time to open on shift. A prescription goes home with a family who'd read it more easily in Urdu.

I usually start with whatever bothers me: a clinical workflow, a messy dataset, a system I don't quite follow. I take it apart until the logic is visible, then build the tool I wish had existed. Antibiotogram, for one, started because I was tired of flipping through PDFs on duty.

Engineering turns those questions into useful systems. Sometimes the problem turns out not to have been mine alone.

PL. 2.1 Data & intelligence

MeritNama

Medical induction, understood.

Deployed · landing public, analytics app invite-only

Problem
Punjab's residency induction runs on merit, preferences and quotas across many rounds. Its history is spread across gazette notifications and merit-list files that are hard to compare.
Approach
Turn each official merit list and gazette into structured data, normalise scores against each year's policy, and simulate seat allocation round by round from a candidate's own preference list.
Today
A public landing page. The merit tables, predictor, calculator and allocation simulator sit behind invite-only sign-in. The data covers inductions 8 to 21.
Next question
What would hospital-level profiles show that a specialty cutoff hides?

PL. 2.2 Clinical systems

Nabz

Prescriptions a parent can actually read.

Released · v0.2.0, PWA and Windows clinic station

Give 5 ml twice a day after food for 7 days 7 دن تک کھانے کے بعد دن میں دو بار 5 ملی لیٹر دیں

Problem
A prescription has two readers: the pharmacist checking the clinical record, and the family who takes it home and may read Urdu more easily than English.
Approach
One structured record writes both. English clinical sections, and patient instructions in Nastaʿlīq Urdu with their own word order, never machine-translated. Text is shaped with HarfBuzz and printed at exact millimetre sizes.
Today
An installable PWA plus a single-file Windows clinic station. Records stay on the doctor's device, and the server refuses clinical fields. There's a test that checks this against a live server.
Next question
How much of a specialty fits in a content pack: a file, not a fork?

PL. 2.3 Clinical systems · Data

Antibiome

What is happening locally?

Released · Antibiome PICU 0.1.3 for Windows

Problem
A paediatric ICU records admissions, cultures, antibiotics and vital signs every day. Very little of it comes back as something the unit can learn from.
Approach
Fast entry on the unit PC or paired phones, therapy recorded as timed episodes, then PIM3 severity, days of therapy, WHO AWaRe and a first-isolate antibiogram, all computed locally in SQLite.
Today
A desktop app for Windows 10/11 and 7/8.1. The original NICU culture web app has been retired and is kept only so units can export their data.
Next question
What changes when a unit writes its antibiotic guideline from its own data, month by month?

Antibiotogram

See the antibiotic pattern.

Live as "Antibiotic Hub" · last updated April 2026

Problem
On a busy shift, choosing an empirical antibiotic meant flipping through long guideline PDFs.

Live on GitHub Pages as an educational aid. It's a guide, not a substitute for clinical judgement or a local antibiogram.

PL. 2.5 Local experiments

Nisbat

A family's day, on every screen.

Deployed · Android app and Chromecast TV, active October 2026

Problem
A Pakistani household's day runs on prayer times, prices, weather and smog, and the information is scattered across a dozen apps and sites.
Approach
One calm dashboard for the living-room TV and every phone. Urdu throughout, official PBS grocery prices, AQI guidance and prayer reminders, on the family's own home server.
Today
A product page, an Android app and a TV receiver. Model estimates are labelled as estimates.
Next question
Which local data sources are reliable enough to show a family without a caveat?

PL. 2.6 Community & play

Shaheen

A clan that needed a memory.

Deployed as BRAWLISTAN · data refreshed daily

Problem
A Pakistani Brawlhalla clan had players, rivalries and ratings, and nowhere to keep any of it.
Approach
A Discord bot that links players to the official Brawlhalla API, snapshots ratings on a schedule and runs challenges and brackets, plus a public site that reads from the same data.
Today
Live as BRAWLISTAN, "Pakistan's Brawlhalla Network". If the home server is off, the site falls back to committed snapshots. Design decisions are kept as ADRs.
Next question
Can a ladder stay fair while many of its players haven't claimed their profiles yet?

03 / The method

How I work

A loop, not a list of services.

Observe

Start where the friction is: a ward round, a merit list, a printed sheet. Write down what actually happens, not what the process says should happen.

From the archiveNabz's queue sync was rebuilt after a live probe showed a payment silently reverting when two stations disagreed.

Understand

Go back to the primary source and learn the rules the system really runs on, edge cases included.

From the archiveMeritNama reads official PHF gazettes and merit lists, policy by policy, and doesn't rely on forum estimates.

Model

Shape the data before the screen. A good model makes the wrong thing hard to record.

From the archiveAntibiome PICU's design document starts from an encounter spine (admission, episodes, discharge) and only then describes screens.

Build

Build the smallest thing that runs where the work happens, on the machines that are actually there.

From the archiveAntibiome ships Windows 7 builds; Nabz's clinic station is one self-contained executable.

Test

Test the promise as well as the function. If a tool says data stays local, prove it.

From the archiveA Nabz server test posts clinical content to a live server and asserts that nothing reaches the disk.

Improve

Version the definitions, record the decisions and change things in the open.

From the archiveAntibiome labels its MDR definition by version, and BRAWLISTAN keeps its design decisions as numbered ADRs.

04 / Field notes

Beyond the screen

What keeps turning up in the work.

  1. N° 01 · Clinical medicine

    Most of the work starts on a ward. Paediatrics keeps coming back: a paediatric ICU, WHO and CDC growth charts, a paediatrics viva pilot.

  2. N° 02 · Language

    Urdu isn't a translation layer. Nabz writes patient instructions in Nastaʿlīq with their own word order, and Nisbat runs its whole interface right to left.

  3. N° 03 · Data you can read

    Data is far more useful once someone can actually read it. A heatmap beats a wall of tables, and a cited page beats a confident answer.

  4. N° 04 · Local problems

    Prayer times, official grocery prices, smog readings, induction rounds in Punjab. The problems are local, so the tools are too.

  5. N° 05 · Play

    A Brawlhalla clan needed a bot, an API and a ranking site. Systems thinking doesn't switch off after the shift.

  6. N° 06 · A few things learned

    • A complicated system gets easier once you can see its pieces.
    • Good tools often start as selfish projects.
    • Not every experiment needs to become a product.

Tools I reach for Python · TypeScript & JavaScript · SQL · HTML/CSS. Whatever else a problem needs, I learn.

Can a study tool show its sources the way a good teacher does?

medNAMA answers from standard medical textbooks with the book and page cited inline, drills on recalled FCPS Part 1 papers and re-tests whatever you got wrong. A viva pilot for FCPS-II and IMM Paediatrics candidates is running alongside it.

It's running with a small group of trainees right now. Access is by invite, requested through a GitHub issue.
